### 2. Commanding Chaos #1 — Emergent Battle Readability

A QA and Design investigation into how large-scale medieval battles can remain chaotic without feeling random or unreadable.

Using publicly available material from *Chronicles: Medieval* as a reference point, the study examines:

* player commands and AI interpretation;
* formation behaviour under pressure;
* casualties, morale, rout and retreat;
* cause-and-effect readability;
* systemic risk and reproducibility;
* debugging and observability requirements;
* feature-level and system-level test coverage.

The central question is:

> **How can a player command chaos if they cannot understand it?**

**Demonstrates:** design-intent analysis · emergent systems · AI testing · observability · test strategy

## How I Approach QA

I approach QA as a way to **reduce uncertainty, expose risk and help teams make better decisions**.

Finding an issue is only the beginning. I want to understand:

* why it happens;
* which conditions make it reproducible;
* which systems are involved;
* how it affects the player or product;
* how widely the risk may spread;
* what evidence helps the team act on it.

When behaviour is unclear, I investigate. When a problem is complex, I break it into observable states and dependencies. When several systems interact, I look beyond the isolated feature and test whether the complete experience remains coherent, stable and understandable.

My Game Design background supports this approach: quality is not only functional correctness, but also **clarity, reliability, usability, balance and player trust**.
